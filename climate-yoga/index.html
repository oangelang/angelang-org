Traditional Yoga Practice

Two yamas to start from.

We come at climate from yoga, using breath, body and movement to learn. Two yamas, the first of yoga's eight limbs, set the terms here.

Ahimsa

Yama: non-harming

It starts inward. No forcing. No judging what the body cannot yet do. Then it extends outward to the ecosystems we are trying to protect.

Aparigraha

Yama: non-possessiveness

We need nothing but ourselves. No mat, no studio, no membership. Just breath and ground.

Grounding

It means two things at once.

  1. BodyReconnecting to your own physical body.
  2. ScreensStepping back from the technology that usually mediates your relationship to everything else.

Before we take on the climate, the systems and the screens, we relearn something more basic: what it feels like to be in a body, on the ground, breathing.

From there we work breath by breath toward what is sustainable, what has longevity, and what will hold up for our minds, our bodies and the ecosystems we live inside of. It is a practice you return to daily, the same way you return to your breath. Over time it gets easier and more natural, and the benefits compound.

After the session

Carry it back to the policy table.

Each session ends with a share-out of guided questions, resources and thoughts on each topic. Take these three back to your desk.

  • What did your body notice that your head had missed?
  • What felt sustainable enough to repeat tomorrow?
  • How could we teach and think about this topic differently?
