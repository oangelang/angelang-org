Humanity in the Room (formerly AI for Good NYC)

Problem:
Institutional leaders are being pushed to adopt AI but rarely sit in the same room as the people who build it or the communities it affects.
My role:
Co-founded with Paulina Munoz (Do Good Capital). Convene events, run a weekly newsletter, and advise organizations deciding how to use AI.

Events and a newsletter that bring together engineers, AI safety researchers, nonprofit leaders, funders, and social entrepreneurs.

Global health and evidence

Phone surveys for maternal health in rural India

Problem:
Measuring maternal and child health knowledge by face-to-face survey is expensive. Phone surveys are cheaper but their reliability was unknown in rural India.
My role:
First author. Led the analyses, wrote the manuscript.
Approach:
Cross-sectional study in four districts of Madhya Pradesh. A face-to-face baseline survey (5,095 pregnant women), then a test-retest face-to-face subsample (n=205) and caller-attended phone interviews (n=375).
Result:
Population-level knowledge estimates were similar by phone and in person.
Limits:
Data collected pre-COVID. Phone ownership and literacy have changed substantially since then, and reliability for individual women was poor, so phone surveys suit population estimates, not individual tracking.
BMJ Open, 2022 Full text (PMC)

Funded by the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation.

Folia Water and the Drinkable Book

Problem:
Billions lack safe drinking water, and filters were hard to distribute and use in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs).
My role:
Product engineer and microbiologist (2015 to 2017). Ran microbiology evaluation of the filter paper, then product and interaction design, field pilots, and business models.
Approach:
Silver nanoparticle filter paper with multilingual materials, tested in field pilots in Bangladesh and South Africa.
Result:
NSF Nanosystems ERC Best Poster Award, 2016. Product covered by international press (see below).
Limits:
Individual water filters address downstream symptoms. Upstream fixes (infrastructure, regulation, governance) are what governments and institutions should provide. Household education alone cannot solve systemic access failures.
BBC, 2015 CNN, 2015 Time, 2015 NPR, 2015 Washington Post, 2015 Smithsonian CMU Today

Experiential learning: Norway and Bhutan

Norway:
Planned an expedition examining the paradox of a nation celebrated for green credentials while simultaneously being a major gas exporter and pursuing deep-sea mining. Through interviews and surveys with Norwegian citizens, students explored how people reconcile environmental leadership with fossil fuel production. The investigation surfaced contrasts with Qatar, another energy exporter facing different perceptions despite similar resource extraction. Participants gained critical insights into how national sustainability narratives are constructed, challenging simplistic notions of what makes a country "green."
Bhutan:
Led a journey centered on Gross National Happiness and Bhutan's achievement of carbon negativity. Students witnessed the kingdom's commitment to environmental protection through growing electric vehicle infrastructure and preservation of cultural traditions that support sustainable living. Seeing how Bhutan prioritizes wellbeing over GDP and maintains forest coverage while developing sustainably proved transformative.
Result:
Returning to CMU-Q, students channeled their learning into campus improvements: a vegetable garden, composting systems, and sound healing practices, directly translating Bhutanese principles of holistic wellbeing.
